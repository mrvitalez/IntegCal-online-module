✎Problems

  1. (Sequences, §11.1) Determine whether each sequence converges or diverges, and find the limit if it converges.
    (a) \(a_n = \ln(3n+1) - \ln(n+2)\)   (b) \(a_n = \sqrt{n^2+3n} - n\)   (c) \(a_n = \dfrac{(-1)^n \sin n}{n}\)

  2. (Series, §11.2) Find the sum of each series, or explain why it diverges.
    (a) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{4^n + 2}{5^n}\)   (b) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \ln\frac{n^2}{(n-1)(n+1)}\)   (c) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{2n}}{3^{n+1}}\)

  3. (Applied, §11.2) A ball is dropped from a height of 8 m. Each time it hits the floor it bounces back up to \(\tfrac35\) of the height it fell from. Find the total vertical distance the ball travels, assuming it keeps bouncing forever.

  4. (Integral Test, §11.3) Use the Integral Test to decide whether \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n e^{-n^2}\) converges. Check all hypotheses of the test.

  5. (Estimating sums, §11.3) Compute \(s_4\) for \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^5}\) and use the integral bounds to find an interval that must contain the sum \(s\). Give your answers to five decimal places.

  6. (Comparison tests, §11.4) Determine whether each series converges or diverges. Name the test and the series you compare with.
    (a) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2 + \cos n}{n^2}\)   (b) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{\sqrt{n^4+1}}\)   (c) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sqrt{n}}{n^2 - n + 3}\)

  7. (Alternating series, §11.5) Show that \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^4}\) converges. How many terms are needed to guarantee an error less than 0.0005? Compute that partial sum to five decimal places.

  8. (Ratio and Root Tests, §11.6) Determine whether each series converges or diverges.
    (a) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{10^n}{n!}\)   (b) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n^n}{3^n\, n!}\)   (c) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{n}{2n+1}\right)^{2n}\)

  9. (Strategy, §11.6-11.7) Classify each series as absolutely convergent, conditionally convergent, or divergent, and justify your choice of test.
    (a) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n n}{n^2+1}\)   (b) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin n}{n^2}\)   (c) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n}\)   (d) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-3)^n}{n^2\, 2^n}\)

  10. (Power series, §11.8) Find the radius and interval of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n (x-4)^n}{n\, 3^n}\).

  11. (Functions as power series, §11.9) (a) Find a power series for \(\dfrac{1}{1+4x^2}\) and its radius of convergence. (b) Integrate it to obtain a power series for \(\tan^{-1}(2x)\). (c) Differentiate the geometric series to find a power series for \(\dfrac{1}{(1-x)^2}\), and use it to find \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{2^n}\).

  12. (Taylor series, §11.10) Find the Taylor series of \(f(x) = \dfrac1x\) centered at \(a = 2\), in two ways: (i) from the formula \(c_n = f^{(n)}(2)/n!\) and (ii) by rewriting \(\dfrac1x = \dfrac{1}{2 + (x-2)}\) as a geometric series. What is its radius of convergence?

  13. (Using Maclaurin series, §11.10) (a) Use the Maclaurin series of \(\cos x\) to evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\cos 2x - 1 + 2x^2}{x^4}\). (b) Use a Maclaurin series to approximate \(\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sin(x^2)\,dx\) with error less than 0.001.

  14. (Applied, §11.11) The kinetic energy of an object of rest mass \(m\) at speed \(v\) is \(K = mc^2\left[(1 - v^2/c^2)^{-1/2} - 1\right]\). (a) Use the binomial series to show that \(K \approx \tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac38 mv^4/c^2\). (b) Using these two terms, estimate the largest speed (as a fraction of \(c\), and in m/s with \(c = 3.00\times10^8\) m/s) for which the classical formula \(\tfrac12 mv^2\) is within 1% of \(K\).

  15. (Prove / explain) (a) Prove: if \(a_n > 0\) and \(\sum a_n\) converges, then \(\sum a_n^2\) also converges. (b) Explain, with an example, why the converse is false. (c) Explain why the Ratio Test can never decide the convergence of a p-series.
